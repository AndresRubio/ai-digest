Notable themes today

The day’s material splits along a single seam: the thing that got faster, and the thing that got harder to see. On one side, almost every cost curve moved. OpenAI cut Luna 80% and Terra 20%; Google began selling Ironwood TPUs outright at a claimed 50% better performance per dollar; a spot market appeared reselling spare inference at 30–60% off; Spotify cut Claude Code tokens ~90% by routing bulk reads to a cheaper model; ripwire and Firecrawl’s anydoc both attack the same bill by handing agents a deterministic artifact instead of making them reconstruct one. On the other side, the observability that would tell you whether any of it worked got worse. GPT-6 Astra’s recurrent depth buys capability by spending chain-of-thought legibility, on the model already rated Critical. Prompt injection moved into tool output, where neither the input screen nor the action screen is looking. Two runs sharing a provider, model, metric and benchmark name can still disagree, because the label names a dataset family rather than a measurement procedure — and when Artificial Analysis made its agent index harder, Gemini 3.8 Flash fell from 89% to 19%. The two sides meet in the patch numbers: 6,080 AI-generated security patches against six CVEs, and only 26% fixed the bug cleanly. Generation is now cheap enough to be nearly free and adjudication is not, which is the same asymmetry the seven autonomous businesses demonstrated by spending thousands on inference, earning $0, and having to be stopped for sending spam. The constructive answers all share a shape, and it is not a better model: precedent gaps rather than content filters, QuickJS sandboxes rather than trusted output, per-fact provenance rather than a dataset-level attestation, and — in the sharpest formulation of the week — the FDA’s reproducibility bar, which a hosted frontier model cannot meet at all, because it is neither deterministic across runs nor still there in two years.

Notable themes — second run

Against that, the claims that came from the labs themselves got harder to check rather than easier: OpenAI's Navier–Stokes proof arrived with 130 billion output tokens behind it and an unresolved question about whether a mathematician's unpublished drafts were in the training data; OpenAI, Anthropic and Z.ai each withheld a model on capability grounds no outsider can audit; and the encrypted reasoning traces everyone assumed were opaque turned out to be replayable into a weaker sibling and decodable in plaintext. The through-line is that the industry is getting better at measuring the scaffolding and worse at verifying the models.
