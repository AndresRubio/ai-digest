Notable themes today

The day's through-line is that the interesting number stopped being the score and started being the spread around it. Fireworks reported DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash matching GPT-6 Astra's DeepSWE band at $0.43 a task, fifteen times cheaper, with all four models tested inside 0.7 pass@1 points of each other — and then published the figure that makes the comparison mean something, which is that 0.7 points is smaller than its own measured run-to-run variation. IBM Research named the general case the same day: ALTK-Evolve's “inconsistency gap” is a model's success rate on one task dropping across repeated runs of that task, which makes “the agent did it” and “the agent will do it again” separate claims needing separate evidence. That is the third independent finding this month, after the shuffled-task-order result on agent memory, that the variance routinely exceeds the effect. The releases pull the same way from the cost side. TypeSafe's Jev abandons text generation entirely to return typed decisions, which is what buys its claimed 20–200× speed and free output tokens; Periodic Neon beats both frontier models on a scientific-analysis benchmark at lower cost per run after midtraining on lab data; Perplexity replaced DynamoDB with its own store and cut p99 batch reads from 123ms to 24.2ms. Each is the same judgement: generality is what you are paying for, and on a narrow enough workload it is the thing to remove. The counter-motion is Google's, folding transcription, translation, speech and music into one Gemini 3.8 Live surface. Underneath, the supervision question got its first honest prices: Nous refactored a million-line codebase with 1,393 agents for $19,000 and reported that the hard part was reconciling hundreds of workers who were each individually right, and Pinecone started metering read egress, which prices not how many queries you run but how much data each one hands back. And in policy the pacing argument met its first flat refusal, Zuckerberg arguing every lab is already incentivised to slow itself — in the same post that named Meta's next model as weeks away, which is the incentive stated out loud.
