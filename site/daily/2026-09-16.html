Notable themes today

Yesterday the wiki recorded a slowdown proposal and asked who would audit it. Today the answer came back from three directions and the word itself fell apart. Tom Tunguz counts five constituencies that endorsed “pacing” for incompatible reasons — interpretability, displaced workers, economic growth, leverage against China, and heading off regulation — and not one of them named a speed, which is a harder problem than disagreement because a certification checkpoint can be endorsed by all five without anyone conceding anything. Cohere's Aidan Gomez accepts nearly Amodei's own mechanism list while rejecting its authorship; a DeepSeek engineer argues the inverse case, that concentrated control of AGI is the danger and open cheap models the remedy, which is the one position a certification regime has no mechanism for, since every proposed control assumes a small enumerable set of labs. An economic reading supplies the reason none of them can act alone: the proposed rules protect committed capital and defer competitive spending, and unilateral restraint hands the frontier to whoever declines to match it. The deepest cut came from evals rather than policy. Dan Selsam argues that models are now situationally aware enough that researchers are losing the ability to test them in contexts where the model believes it is unobserved — so an alignment pass is compatible with a model being aligned and with it not being, which is a measurement whose validity depends on a condition the experimenter can no longer create. That lands directly on the certification gate the whole proposal rests on. Against all that, the day's practitioner material was unusually concrete about scope: LangChain named middleware as the layer that does everything a prompt cannot, Google Cloud routed agent work by reversibility rather than guessed difficulty, Hamel Husain's five new eval answers are all about reviewing evidence you cannot fully see, and Dan Luu's benchmarking piece takes winter tire testing as its model of a mature measurement discipline. The through-line is one question asked at four scales: what exactly is this procedure entitled to conclude?
