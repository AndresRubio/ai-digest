Applications & case studies

Last updated: 2026-09-17

Current state

Today's deployments all answer the same question in the same way: the model was never the missing piece, the encoded knowledge was. Pinecone published the sharpest numbers on this page for a support agent after adding Nexus, a curated artifact the agent queries on every ticket — eligible tickets reaching the agent up from 76.5% to 94.2%, tickets it meaningfully assists on from 60.5% to 87.8%, and tickets closed with no person involved from 24.6% to 55.1%. Nexus holds the join keys, the freshness caveats and the queries that turn a vague report into an answerable one: what a support engineer knows and what was written down nowhere. That is why the assist rate and the unassisted-closure rate rose together rather than trading off, and why the finding generalises — retrieval over the documentation was never the blocker, because the documentation never held the operational knowledge. Anthropic's Salesforce in Claude beta is the packaged form of the same bet, putting CRM records, pipeline actions, Slack deal context and 37 named sales skills inside the assistant, where the skill count is a claim about decomposing a job rather than about the model. Its unexamined half is the write path, since an assistant that updates pipeline records is altering what other processes read as ground truth. And Taste Labs' Brand API adds the check that design agents have lacked: extract brand context, search a curated index, and verify output against the brand before it ships — making conformance a result rather than a hope, though nothing published says what conformance is measured against.

The prior cycle's deployment news was about reach rather than capability. Roblox (Sep 11) widened its generative game-creation feature to Serbia and Singapore, added desktop access, promised a prompt-based scene generator later this year, expanded NPC capabilities, and made games playable outside Roblox itself, with a creator card and wallet alongside. Prompt-to-content for a large amateur creator base is not new; taking down the walls around where the output runs is, and it converts generated games from platform-locked features into portable artifacts — the structural equivalent of what the licensed-catalogue music deals below achieve by fixing rights instead of reach. The rollout method transfers even if the product does not: generative creation is being released one market at a time, the way a risky deployment is staged, rather than switched on everywhere at once.

The week's substantial piece is a measurement problem, not a model one. A survey of the healthcare literature (Sep 12) totals up what analytics built on codes alone cannot see: at a London teaching hospital a year after a full Epic rollout, 516 patients' problem lists held 2,841 diagnoses and review of the notes found 1,722 more, so 37.7% existed only in free text; administrative-only HEDIS rates ran 20.4 points below chart-reviewed rates across 283 plans; Medicare claims coded 2.01% of beneficiaries as smokers where the BRFSS survey found 10.03%; the National Inpatient Sample codes obesity at 15.4% against measured BMI's 41.9%; 14.4% of a Kaiser cohort with eGFR between 10 and 59 carried a CKD code. The explanation is structural rather than sloppy — a code is produced at billing time under coding rules, so its presence is decent evidence a condition exists and its absence is weak evidence it does not. What makes this current rather than a standing grievance is the direction ambient scribes push it: across 20,302 matched primary-care visits, AI-scribed notes documented significantly more neuropsychiatric symptoms while the odds of a resulting referral, diagnosis code or prescription fell (adjusted OR 0.83 against unscribed visits; 0.97 for human scribes). Notes are growing roughly 20% per visit and the coded fraction is not, so the gap widens precisely where AI is being deployed. The requirements this implies for extraction — assertion and temporal handling so “denies” and “history of” are not counted as active, provenance to the source sentence, running on identified data inside the customer's environment, deterministic measure logic on top of extracted facts — are the same constraints the fact-level provenance piece named a week earlier, arrived at from the audit side instead of the measurement side. Elsewhere, Universal Music and ElevenLabs announced a licensed-catalogue remix platform (Sep 11), the second licensing-first launch in a fortnight after Suno v6's Warner and BMG deal — the binding constraint on generative music having moved from model quality to catalogue rights.

Before this cycle, three items shared a through-line: the model was the easy part in all of them. A Sep 10 analysis proposes personalised cancer-vaccine design as the cleanest available test of whether genome language models learned transferable biology or fitted their corpus: five steps demanding different knowledge — identify a patient's mutations, predict which peptides are presented, predict which provoke a response, rank the targets, design the encoding RNA — each of which must be right for the output to be a candidate medicine, and no population average for a wrong answer to hide inside, because the target is one person. PostHog's Replay Vision makes the point from the engineering side: letting a model watch a session recording sounds like a model capability, but replays are DOM mutation streams rather than video, so the shippable work was a custom rasterizer running hundreds of concurrent jobs across many pods — over 370 years of recorded sessions turned into roughly 3.5 million videos since March. And Suno v6 (Sep 9) settles this page's longest-deferred question with money rather than argument: trained on music licensed from Warner, BMG and other majors, and shipped as a replacement for the previous models rather than a compliant tier beside them, which puts a revealed price on the training-data answer that every other media-generation vendor can now read.

Earlier cycles

Before them, biology took the cycle's largest step by changing distribution rather than capability. DeepMind's AlphaGenome Atlas ships one petabyte of precomputed predictions covering the regulatory effect of all 9 billion possible single-nucleotide human variants, each with a single impact score, queryable without writing code or running the model — which removes the inference budget as a prerequisite for using it, and that was the actual barrier for most labs. GenBio's AIDO Cell attacks the other half of the problem, simulating a cell's natural state and its responses to successive perturbations for K562 and HepG2, with an academic early-access programme forming; following predicted consequences across biological levels one intervention at a time is exactly what wet-lab work does slowly. Outer Biosciences is the early, unvalidated third entry, keeping donated human skin tissue alive and training on how it responds to compounds — a training signal from live human tissue rather than published assays, with no model or benchmark published yet.

In professional services the pattern is memory rather than modelling. Harvey II lets legal agents start each task with the context, memory and preferences accumulated from prior ones, which targets the actual cost in that work: the same matter, client conventions and document set re-explained every session. Harvey also post-trained a Kimi K3 base with asynchronous RL inside long-horizon legal environments and routes specialized diligence, review-table and firm-knowledge capabilities as tools — all vendor-reported. a16z's adoption data points the same way from outside any one vendor: since February the fastest-growing Codex adopters have been non-engineering functions, with legal up 108×, sales and recruiting 41×, marketing 26× and healthcare 24×, which are multiples off small bases but describe knowledge work being reshaped into agent-shaped tasks rather than code being written. And one anecdote is worth keeping for what it says about where expertise now binds: an Anthropic employee whose formal mathematics ended after one semester of high-school geometry used the Claude phone app, prompting mostly “keep going,” and produced a Riemann-adjacent result a Stanford number theorist called the most impressive AI mathematics result so far.

Two of this cycle's items are about what has to be true for an AI system to be admissible rather than impressive. The FDA's December 2025 real-world-evidence guidance, operational since February 2026, treats relevance and reliability as per-fact properties: a reviewer asking where a single value came from should get the source document, the exact span, the model and prompt version, the extraction confidence, the conflicts detected and the rule or reviewer that resolved them. Meeting that takes 42 distinct capabilities across ingestion, extraction, privacy, reasoning, audit and versioning — two to three years of engineering — and several of them, notably per-fact source coordinates and point-in-time reconstruction, only work if designed in from the first sprint. The consequence that generalizes past healthcare is about model choice: a hosted frontier model is neither deterministic across runs nor pinnable across years, so an extraction step run through one cannot be reproduced when that version is retired, and a peer-reviewed comparison puts a healthcare-specific small model at 96% F1 on PHI detection against 79% for GPT-4o at over 80% lower cost. Alongside it, Rentosertib — designed with AI for idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis — showed reductions in biological age markers across six “aging clocks”, themselves ML models predicting morbidity and mortality; a secondary observation, years from approval even for its original indication. On the generative side, Runway's Solaris pushes interface generation past markup entirely, producing real-time interactive interfaces frame by frame with rendering and interaction handled jointly and no intermediate representation, aimed as much at training agents in environments that change under them as at building UIs. And OpenAI's Rosalind Workbench packages life-science tooling and specialized biology models into the ChatGPT app as a research preview — the same bet as Claudeforce, aimed at a field where the tooling, not the model, is the barrier.

Before all of that, video was where retrieval went agentic. Gemini's agentic video understanding stops sampling at a fixed frame rate and lets the model choose what to inspect and through which channel — frames, audio, or transcript — skimming, searching, zooming and rewatching only where the question requires it, for up to 88% fewer tokens, 66% lower cost and 7% better accuracy. The win comes from not ingesting most of the input, which is the same principle behind the agentic-RAG work this page has tracked in text. Alongside it, World Labs' Atlas adds precise camera-path control to world generation and reconstructs scenes from sparse images into point clouds and Gaussian splats — artifacts a robotics, VFX or game pipeline can consume, as opposed to a rendered clip it cannot.

The strongest evidence yet that a chatbot can do measurable clinical good in one sitting arrived Aug 12: a randomized controlled trial of overit, a Claude Sonnet 4.5 app for post-breakup distress, found a single ~20-minute conversation cut Breakup Distress Scale scores from 35.3 to 26.6 after seven days against 35.9 to 32.2 in the control arm, still separated at one month, with 61.7% of users reporting a “sudden insight” against 19.3% of controls (n=171). Single-session matters because multi-session therapeutic chatbots lose users to attrition before the benefit lands. The transferable engineering is the two-call structure: every user turn triggers one model call to judge which of five therapeutic milestones the conversation has reached and a separate one to generate the reply, which is what keeps the model challenging a self-limiting belief rather than sycophantically affirming it — a template for any agent that has to push back on the person it is talking to.

The newest entry (Aug 3) opens a product-search thread: Onton's Ontology 1 is a neurosymbolic e-commerce search model that doesn't trust seller labels — there is no product filter for "pet-friendly," so it reasons from properties more likely to be objective (fiber, weave, construction) inside an explicit, inspectable knowledge graph, and flags claims the product data contradicts. On Subtext-Decor-90, scored by three independent LLM judges, it reaches P@10 0.630 against Google Shopping's 0.543 and Amazon's 0.469 while indexing roughly 1% of their catalogs, winning 52 of 90 queries outright (judge agreement is modest at Krippendorff's alpha 0.465, though all three judges rank the engines identically). Live on Onton.com; no public API or open weights. It is the clearest applied datapoint yet that explicit, inspectable reasoning can beat scale in a domain where the incumbent advantage is catalog size.

Before that, the newest healthcare entry (Aug 1) reframes a familiar risk: clinical LLMs don't just make novel errors, they inherit one of the best-studied problems in patient safety — cognitive bias in diagnosis. Give a frontier model a clinical note about a 52-year-old with chest pain and its recommendation can be steered by the note's framing, the same anchoring effect documented in physicians since Graber, Franklin, and Gordon's 2005 diagnostic-error work. Because models learn from clinician-written notes, the bias arrives with the training data; it isn't a problem AI invented, and (as the July 31 evals thread argued) it isn't caught by knowledge-style benchmark passes either.

Before that, AI-assisted drug discovery entered the page (July 31): Stanford University researchers, using AI to analyze human protein data, identified a naturally-occurring peptide that delivers results comparable to Ozempic (the GLP-1 obesity drug) while reducing side effects — a non-synthetic alternative surfaced by model-driven analysis of existing biological data rather than synthetic chemistry, and a concrete instance of AI shortcutting the discovery step of the pharma pipeline.

The Maven Clinic case study below now has a sequel that shows what happened after launch. William Horton returned to the Vanishing Gradients podcast (July 25) four months after Maven Assistant's initial rollout: the healthcare agent is now available to 100% of users, up from the 20% it launched to, weekly conversation volume has grown roughly tenfold, and real usage overturned part of the original roadmap — 50-60% of early conversations turned out to be basic health questions rather than the provider-search and appointment-booking tools the team had built out first. The team also moved from Gemini Flash models toward newer OpenAI models, now runs over 1,000 deterministic test scenarios alongside LLM judges, and treats a model swap itself as a trigger to re-audit the harness rather than assume improvement — a newer model, in one case, correctly flagged a mocked "upcoming" appointment that had already happened, which is a good outcome but still a change in behavior the team had to go verify rather than take on faith. It's a rare look at the gap between launch-day assumptions and four months of real conversation data, and a concrete argument for the "convert production failures into the eval suite" practice the original case study described.

The same week, John Snow Labs' Medical LLM – Medium ranked first on all 15 clinical/biomedical benchmarks in the MedHELM suite (July 25), averaging 80.9 against 76.5, 75.0, and 74.7 for the newest OpenAI, Anthropic, and Google frontier releases respectively. It runs on a single GPU with per-server annual licensing (no per-token cost), modeled at roughly 12-29x cheaper than frontier APIs at population scale (1 million patients) — directly extending the deployment-economics cost model already on this page — though margins over frontier models are near-zero on saturated exam-style benchmarks and widest on operational tasks (clinical-note error-catching, medication Q&A), a useful caveat against reading a single average score as uniform superiority.

The earlier strand below is production agent architecture in a regulated, high-stakes domain: William Horton (Staff AI Engineer, Maven Clinic) described how Maven Assistant, a healthcare agent for Maven Clinic's women's and family-health platform, was built and evaluated the day after it reached its first external users. The design keeps the toolset small on purpose — a lead agent routes to four narrower specialists (appointments, provider search, health questions, Maven support) covering just 15-20 tools total, with hard guardrails running before dynamic routing — and it ships with a consequence-scaled release bar rather than one pass rate for everything: a wrong tool call might be accepted at nine-out-of-ten passes, but answers that could influence tens of thousands of dollars in benefits, or self-harm language, are withheld or routed straight to a human. The practice worth generalizing is turning production failures directly into the eval suite — a bad trace becomes a regression case, deterministic code checks tool calls, and LLM judges (calibrated against human labels) cover behavior that can't be reduced to an exact expected output. This slots alongside the existing healthcare-economics and evaluation-realism threads below as a third healthcare-AI angle: not cost, and not benchmark validity, but how a real deployed healthcare agent is architected and kept safe to ship.

Several other strands of applied work remain visible. The next is deployment economics: a detailed cost model for patient-level healthcare AI argues that, at production scale, the deployment model (local vs. frontier API) dwarfs the choice between API vendors — locally deployed medical LLMs run ~1.35× cheaper at pilot volume but ~18–29× cheaper at a million patients, because per-token pricing is linear in data volume while per-server licensing plus infrastructure grows sublinearly. The same source has since widened the healthcare thread from cost to evaluation realism, arguing that headline "medical benchmark" wins for general-purpose models reflect single-turn question-answering rather than the multi-step, record-grounded clinical work that decides deployment value (see Evals & benchmarks for the methodology dispute). A third healthcare signal is on the data side: NeuroVFM (University of Michigan, Nature Medicine) shows a generalist brain-imaging model can be trained on 5.24M uncurated clinical MRI/CT volumes with no labels — a template for turning the idle scan archives every hospital already holds into open foundation models, and the clearest sign yet that medical AI's labeled-data bottleneck is loosening (method detail on the Research papers page). Speech remains a strong applied area on both ends of the pipeline: on generation, Miso Labs' MisoTTS brings emotive open-weights text-to-speech (~110ms latency, one-shot voice cloning); on transcription, two production-grade speech-to-text releases — Microsoft's MAI-Transcribe-1.5 (2.4% WER, 43 languages, an hour of audio in under 15 seconds, on Azure AI Foundry) and NVIDIA's Nemotron 3.5 ASR (600M streaming, 40 locales, fully local via ONNX) — make fast, multilingual transcription a commodity building block.

Real-time translation is the newest branch: Gradium's stt-translate and s2s-translate collapse the usual ASR→translation→TTS stack into single end-to-end models streaming across five languages, reportedly beating gpt-realtime-translate on both accuracy and latency — a ready benchmark target for multilingual voice agents. The build-vs-buy decision behind all of this got a systematic framework July 27, when Hamza Farooq's voice-agent deep dive argued every architectural choice — cascading STT→LLM→TTS pipelines vs. native speech-to-speech models (OpenAI's Realtime API, Google's Gemini Live), open self-hosted components (Whisper, Kokoro) vs. closed APIs — is really a decision about how to spend a roughly 700-800ms per-turn latency budget, with speech-to-speech buying latency at the cost of the inspection and compliance checkpoints a cascading pipeline provides; the piece also flags a widening trust gap, with consumer preference for human agents over AI reportedly climbing even as blind listening tests show AI voices are now largely indistinguishable from human ones.

On creative media, the open stack keeps filling in (Stability AI audio, ByteDance unified video). On physical AI, NVIDIA's Cosmos 3 open world model targets robotics, AVs, and warehouse monitoring. On knowledge work, a Perplexity/Harvard study quantifies the agent-vs-chat productivity gap: workers using an autonomous agent ("Computer") finished tasks in 87% less time at 94% lower cost than search alone. The common shift: applied AI is moving from generic LLM wrappers to domain-specific agent loops with their own verification — and, increasingly, to deployment choices driven by total cost at scale.

Taste Labs ships a Brand API that lets a design agent check its output before shipping it

Taste Labs launched a Brand API for AI design agents built on three primitives rather than a general “AI for taste” pitch: extract existing brand context, search a curated brand index, and verify generated work against the brand before it ships. The third is the one that makes it more than a style prompt — generation with a brand description in context produces output that is plausibly on-brand and unverifiable, while a separate verification call makes conformance a checkable property with a pass or fail. That is the same structural move as running evaluation outside the model that produced the work.

It is the design counterpart to Inspo’s 832-site reference corpus published the same day — one supplies references before generation, the other a check afterwards. What is unstated is how the verification judges conformance, which is exactly where a brand check either encodes something a designer would recognise as judgement or reduces to colour and font matching.

Voice agent architecture & latency budgets

A single design essay, kept apart because its claim is that the choices are not independent: each one spends part of the same per-turn latency budget. What to check is whether the budget it names holds for your own traffic, and which inspection points a lower-latency path removes.

2026-07-27

Voice agent architecture reframed as a latency-budget allocation problem

The essay argues every voice-agent design choice — cascading STT/LLM/TTS against native speech-to-speech (OpenAI’s Realtime API, Google’s Gemini Live), open self-hosted components (Whisper, Kokoro) against closed APIs — is really an allocation decision inside a roughly 700 to 800 millisecond per-turn budget. Cascading pipelines preserve the inspection points where a transcription error is caught or a compliance violation blocked, and pay for them in latency at every handoff; speech-to-speech removes the handoffs and the checkpoints together. Open components are competitive on quality but carry self-hosting costs that only beat closed APIs past a certain scale.

Its practical guidance is that voice earns its keep in hands-busy, phone-native or accessibility contexts rather than wherever a user could otherwise type, and that the gating question for 2026 deployments is the honest failure modes — noisy-environment transcription errors, barge-in handling, PII exposure — not whether a voice agent can be shipped at all. It also flags a widening trust gap: consumer preference for human agents is reportedly climbing even as blind listening tests show AI voices are largely indistinguishable from human ones.

2026-09-12

Structured data alone misses 37.7% of documented diagnoses — and ambient scribes widen the gap

A survey of the measurement literature puts hard numbers on what claims-and-codes analytics cannot see. At a London teaching hospital a year after a full Epic rollout, 516 patients’ problem lists held 2,841 diagnoses and chart review of the notes found 1,722 more — 37.7% of diagnoses existed only in free text. Notes are growing about 20% per visit and the coded fraction is not, so the gap widens precisely where AI is being deployed. Fact-level provenance: 42 capabilities, and why a hosted API cannot pass the audit

The FDA’s December 2025 real-world-evidence guidance, operational since February 2026, treats relevance and reliability as per-fact properties rather than per-dataset ones: a reviewer asking where a single value came from should get the source document, the exact span, the model and prompt version, the extraction confidence, the conflicts detected and the rule or reviewer that resolved them. Meeting that takes 42 distinct capabilities across ingestion, extraction, privacy, reasoning, audit and versioning — two to three years of engineering — and several of them, notably per-fact source coordinates and point-in-time reconstruction, only work if designed in from the first sprint.

The sharpest architectural consequence generalises well past healthcare: a hosted frontier model is neither deterministic across runs nor pinnable across years, so an extraction step run through one cannot be reproduced when that version has been retired. A peer-reviewed comparison puts a healthcare-specific small model at 96% F1 on PHI detection against 79% for GPT-4o, 83% for AWS Comprehend Medical and 91% for Azure, at over 80% lower cost — which turns “use a small specialist model” from a cost argument into an auditability one.
